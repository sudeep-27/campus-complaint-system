INTRODUCTION TO DATABASE MODELING (ER AND SCHEMA DIAGRAMS)

To establish a clear structural foundation for the Campus Complaint Management System, the database architecture is designed and documented at both a conceptual level and a physical level. The Entity-Relationship (ER) diagram serves as the conceptual model, focusing on the abstract entities (such as users, complaints, and departments), their descriptive attributes, and the logical business relationships connecting them. This conceptual view allows us to map the campus workflows and cardinalities without getting bogged down in database engine specifics. In contrast, the Database Schema Diagram represents the physical implementation, defining the concrete tables, storage engines, data types, index paths, and referential integrity constraints enforced within the MySQL server. Together, these two diagrams bridge the gap between human-centric business logic and machine-level relational structures, ensuring a highly organized, secure, and normalized database layout.
